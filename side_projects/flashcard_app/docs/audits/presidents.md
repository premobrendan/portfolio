# Presidents audit — 45 cards

All 45 cards read in full (front, primary, secondary, bio paragraph, bullets, sources line).

**Ordinal sequence: correct end to end.** Cards 1–21 run 1st–21st. Card 22 Cleveland is "the 22nd and 24th president"; card 23 Benjamin Harrison is 23rd; card 24 McKinley correctly resumes at 25th and the run continues unbroken to Obama at 44th. Card 44 Trump is "the 45th and 47th president" and card 45 Biden is 46th. No cascade error, no duplicate, no gap.

**Currency check (today = September 2026): correct.** Trump's card uses present tense for the second term ("returned to office on January 20, 2025", "His current term has centered on…"); Biden is past tense, 2021–2025; Carter's death is recorded as December 29, 2024. Living presidents (Clinton, G. W. Bush, Obama, Trump, Biden) correctly use "is a Democrat/Republican"; the dead use "was". Nothing is written as though a current thing were past or vice versa.

## Factual errors

- **Zachary Taylor** — "1850: Died on July 9 after sixteen months in office, **the second-shortest presidency**, and was succeeded by Vice President Millard Fillmore" → Taylor's is the **third**-shortest. Garfield served 199 days to Taylor's 492; only W. H. Harrison (31 days) and Garfield were shorter. Fix: "the third-shortest presidency". (Wikipedia, *Zachary Taylor*: "Taylor had the third-shortest presidential term in U.S. history.")

- **Rutherford B. Hayes** — "**1880** - Issued executive orders barring federal officeholders from managing party campaigns, and removed Chester A. Arthur from the New York Custom House." → Both halves are misdated, by two and three years. The executive order was issued in **1877**; Hayes fired Arthur (and Alonzo Cornell) during a congressional recess in **July 1878**, with the replacements confirmed February 1879. Fix: split into a 1877 bullet for the executive order and an 1878 bullet for the removal. (Wikipedia, *Rutherford B. Hayes*: "Hayes issued an executive order that forbade federal office holders from being required to make campaign contributions… Hayes was forced to wait until July 1878, when he fired Arthur and Cornell during a Congressional recess.")

- **Benjamin Harrison** — "**1890** - Signed the Land Revision Act, under which he set aside some 13 million acres as the first forest reserves." → The Land Revision Act (General Revision Act / Forest Reserve Act) was signed **March 3, 1891**. The acreage figure is right. Fix: change the year to 1891. (Wikipedia, *General Revision Act*: "a federal law signed in 1891 by President Benjamin Harrison.")

- **Warren G. Harding** — "**1922**: Signed the Sheppard–Towner Act funding maternal and infant health care." → Signed **November 23, 1921**. Fix: move to the 1921 group. (Wikipedia, *Sheppard–Towner Act*: "signed by President Warren G. Harding on November 23, 1921.")

- **Grover Cleveland** — "A Buffalo lawyer, he **rose from sheriff to mayor to governor of New York in three years** on an anti-corruption reputation." → He took office as sheriff of Erie County on January 1, **1871**, a decade before the rest. The compressed rise is mayor (elected Nov 1881) → governor (elected Nov 1882) → president (elected Nov 1884). Fix: "he rose from mayor of Buffalo to governor of New York to the presidency in three years", or drop "sheriff" from the sequence. (Wikipedia, *Grover Cleveland*: sheriff "took office on January 1, 1871"; "elected mayor of Buffalo in 1881 and governor of New York in 1882.")

- **George Washington** — "1795 — Secured Senate ratification of the Jay Treaty with Britain **and Pinckney's Treaty with Spain**, opening the Mississippi." → The Jay Treaty was ratified by the Senate in June 1795, but Pinckney's Treaty, though signed October 27, 1795, was not ratified by the Senate until **March 7, 1796**. Fix: either move Pinckney's Treaty to a 1796 clause, or reword to "signed in 1795" for Pinckney's. (Wikipedia, *Pinckney's Treaty*: "presented to the United States Senate on February 26, 1796, and… ratified on March 7, 1796.")

- **Martin Van Buren** — "**1821** — Built the Albany Regency machine in New York…" → The Albany Regency is dated from **1822**. Minor, but the bullet is the only anchor for the date. (Wikipedia, *Albany Regency*: "controlled the New York state government between 1822 and 1838.")

- **Harry S. Truman** — "1947: Signed the National Security Act, creating **the Department of Defense**, the CIA and the National Security Council." → The 1947 act created the **National Military Establishment**, which was renamed the Department of Defense by the 1949 amendments. It did create the CIA, the NSC and the office of secretary of defense. Fix: "creating the secretary of defense and the unified military establishment (renamed the Department of Defense in 1949), the CIA and the National Security Council." (Wikipedia, *National Security Act of 1947*.)

- **Franklin Pierce** — "**1853**: Completed the Gadsden Purchase…" → The treaty's first draft was signed December 31, 1853, but it was amended by the Senate in April 1854 and Pierce signed the ratified version in June 1854; "completed" belongs to 1854. Fix: "1853–54". The acreage ("roughly 29,600 square miles", actual 29,640) and the $10 million are right. (Wikipedia, *Gadsden Purchase*.)

- **George W. Bush** — "Signed the **Troubled Asset Relief Program** in October 2008 during the financial crisis." → TARP is a program, not a bill; what he signed was the Emergency Economic Stabilization Act of 2008, which created TARP. Fix: "Signed the Emergency Economic Stabilization Act, creating the Troubled Asset Relief Program, in October 2008."

- **Donald Trump** — "Withdrew the United States from the Paris Agreement **in 2017**…" → He *announced* withdrawal in June 2017; the withdrawal did not take effect until November 4, 2020 (and was repeated in January 2025). Fix: "Announced withdrawal from the Paris Agreement in 2017" or "in 2017, effective 2020".

## Wrong attribution of an act

Nothing found. This is the deck's strongest area: every act I checked near a transition of power is credited to the president who actually did it, including the traps.

- The Morrill Land-Grant Act and the Homestead bill are correctly vetoed by **Buchanan** (1859, 1860) and signed by **Lincoln** (1862) — both cards carry the right half.
- The Perry expedition to Japan is correctly split: **Fillmore** "Dispatched Commodore Matthew Perry" (1852); **Pierce** "Concluded the Treaty of Kanagawa" (1854).
- Texas annexation is **Tyler**'s joint resolution (1845), not Polk's.
- The Department of Labor act, signed March 4, 1913, is correctly credited to **Taft**, not Wilson.
- NAFTA is correctly split: **G. H. W. Bush** "Signed the North American Free Trade Agreement in December 1992, before its ratification under his successor"; **Clinton** "Signed the NAFTA implementation act in 1993."
- The two Independent Treasury Acts are correctly given to **Van Buren** (1840) and **Polk** (1846) as separate laws.
- The Fifteenth Amendment is correctly "Saw ratification of…" under **Grant** rather than "signed".

(The Hayes and Harding items above are wrong *dates*, not wrong presidents — the acts belong to them.)

## Neutrality / framing

- **Donald Trump** — "Impeached a second time in January 2021 and acquitted in February 2021." — The card names the impeachment but never names what it was for. January 6, 2021 and the Capitol attack appear nowhere on the card, nor does the 2020 election loss. This is the deck's most conspicuous omission: elsewhere the deck states causes plainly (Nixon: "Resigned on August 9 amid the Watergate scandal"; A. Johnson: "chiefly for dismissing Secretary of War Edwin Stanton in defiance of that act"). Fix: name the charge — "Impeached a second time in January 2021, for incitement of insurrection after the January 6 attack on the Capitol, and acquitted in February 2021."

- **Ronald Reagan** — "His administration was investigated over the Iran-Contra affair, disclosed in 1986." — Passive and evasive next to how the deck handles other scandals. It says an investigation happened but not what the affair was (arms sales to Iran, proceeds diverted to the Nicaraguan Contras in defiance of the Boland Amendment). Fix: state the substance in one clause, as Grant's card does for the Whiskey Ring.

- **Herbert Hoover** — "raising duties on thousands of imports and **helping to strangle world trade**." — "Strangle" is a verdict, not a description, and it sits in the *secondary*, the line the learner sees first. The bullet version ("provoking retaliatory tariffs abroad") is the neutral phrasing and should be reused. Fix: "…and provoking retaliatory tariffs that deepened the collapse in world trade."

- **James K. Polk** — "Polk pledged to serve a single term and to accomplish four goals, and **achieved them all**… The war he waged against Mexico **enlarged the country by roughly a third**." — Reads as a campaign biography. The war was contested at the time (Whig opposition, Lincoln's Spot Resolutions) and the acquisition reopened the slavery question that Taylor's and Fillmore's cards then have to explain; none of that surfaces. Fix: add one clause noting the war's contested legitimacy, or at least drop "and achieved them all".

- **Chester A. Arthur** — "To general surprise **the consummate spoilsman turned on his own system**, signing the Pendleton Act…" — Novelistic and admiring; the only bio in the deck that narrates a moral arc. Fix: "Though a product of the spoils system, he signed the Pendleton Act…"

- **Martin Van Buren** — "…and **did more than anyone** to build the Democratic Party and the modern party system." — An unverifiable superlative stated as fact. Fix: "and was central to building the Democratic Party and the modern party system."

- **Franklin Pierce** — "**historians consistently rank his term among the least successful**." — An evaluative ranking claim that appears on no other card, including Buchanan's and Andrew Johnson's, which rank as low or lower. Either apply it consistently or cut it.

- **James Buchanan** — Every one of the nine bullets is a failure or a wrong ("Lobbied a Supreme Court justice…", "Met the Panic of 1857 with no federal relief", "Vetoed the Morrill bill", "Vetoed a homestead bill", "Left office with seven states seceded"). The characterization is defensible, but the card reads as a prosecution brief with nothing else in it. Suggest one neutral bullet on his pre-presidential record (the bio already notes it) to break the run.

- **Theodore Roosevelt** — Secondary: "He took the Panama Canal Zone and began building the canal." — Notably terse next to every other secondary in the deck, and "took" is doing loaded work without the supporting facts (the 1903 Panamanian secession, the Hay–Bunau-Varilla Treaty) that the bullets supply. Fix: expand to match the deck's secondary length and specificity.

- **Barack Obama** — The ten bullets are legislative and diplomatic wins with no contested item at all, in a deck that gives Grant, Nixon, Reagan and Clinton their scandals and LBJ his Vietnam escalation. Not an error, but the asymmetry is visible.

- **George W. Bush** — The bio names "the response to the September 11 attacks, the wars in Afghanistan and Iraq, large tax cuts, and the financial crisis"; Hurricane Katrina and the collapse of the Iraq WMD rationale appear nowhere. Lower priority than the Trump omission but the same category.

## Prose issues

- **Hayes, Garfield, Arthur, Cleveland, B. Harrison, McKinley, T. Roosevelt, Taft, Wilson** (nine consecutive cards) — "Born in Delaware, Ohio, on October 4, 1822, **and dead on** January 17, 1893, Rutherford B. Hayes was a Republican…" — "and dead on" is not English in this construction; it reads as a typo for "and died on" and jars in every one of the nine cards that uses it. **Highest-value single prose fix in the deck.**

- **Whole deck — six bio templates applied in blocks, and one construction repeated 23 times.** The opening sentence changes format every nine cards: cards 1–9 "Born in X on DATE, and dying on DATE, NAME was…"; 10–18 "NAME was born in X on DATE, and died on DATE."; 19–27 "Born in X on DATE, and dead on DATE, NAME was…"; 28–36 "NAME (born DATE, in X; died DATE) was…"; 37–40 "Born in X (DATE - DATE), NAME was…"; 41–45 "Born in X on DATE, NAME is a…". Within that, 23 of 45 bios end on "His presidency / His term / His single term / His two terms is/are remembered for" followed by a comma list. The blocks make the deck feel machine-assembled; the "remembered for" tic makes any run of cards feel identical.

- **Whole deck — four different bullet date styles, in the same blocks.** Cards 1–9 use `- 1789 — `; cards 10–18 and 28–36 use `- 1841: `; cards 19–27 use `- 1877 - ` (hyphen); cards 37–45 drop the leading date entirely and bury the year at the end of the sentence ("Signed the Family and Medical Leave Act in 1993."). The last block also breaks the stated deck convention that every bullet carries a dated prefix, and makes the modern cards harder to scan.

- **William Howard Taft** — "1912 - Signed the act creating the Children's Bureau **and the Department of Labor legislation of 1913**." — Does not parse: a bullet dated 1912 whose second half is dated 1913, with "legislation" standing in for a named act. Fix: split into "1912 — Signed the act creating the Children's Bureau" and "1913 — Signed the act creating the Department of Labor on his last day in office."

- **James A. Garfield** — Secondary: "He broke the Stalwart machine's grip on federal patronage by forcing through **his own appointment** to the New York Custom House." — Reads as though Garfield appointed himself collector. He forced through *his own nominee*, William H. Robertson. Fix: "his own nominee for collector of the New York Custom House". ("Broke… grip" is also stronger than the bullet's own account warrants.)

- **Zachary Taylor** — "1850: Died on July 9 after sixteen months in office, the second-shortest presidency, and was succeeded by Vice President Millard Fillmore; his brief tenure left few completed initiatives." — Four clauses and a semicolon doing the work of three bullets, and the last clause repeats what the bio already said. Fix: split, and cut the trailing clause.

- **Benjamin Harrison** — "He won the presidency in 1888 losing the popular vote to Grover Cleveland." — Missing conjunction; the participle dangles. Fix: "He won the presidency in 1888 while losing the popular vote to Grover Cleveland."

- **Jimmy Carter** — "Became the longest-lived US president **before his death in 2024** at age 100." — "Became X before his death" is backwards; he became the longest-lived president in 2019 and held the distinction until he died. Fix: "Became the longest-lived US president in 2019 and died in December 2024 at age 100."

- **Abraham Lincoln** — The bio ends "…**the first American president to be assassinated**" and the last bullet ends "…and died the next morning, **the first president assassinated**." The same fact, in the same words, twice on one card. Fix: cut one. Separately, the bullet list opens at 1862 — Fort Sumter and the outbreak of the war in April 1861 appear only obliquely in the bio, which is a real gap in a list of "notable acts and milestones".

- **Martin Van Buren** — "1839 — Kept the **Aroostook River border dispute** with Britain from becoming a war…" — The episode is the Aroostook War, over the Maine–New Brunswick boundary; "Aroostook River border dispute" is an invented name that will not match anything a learner looks up. Fix: "the Aroostook War, the Maine–New Brunswick boundary dispute".

- **Harry S. Truman** — "1945: Represented the United States at the Potsdam Conference and **signed the United Nations Charter into effect**." — Not what happened and not idiomatic: the US delegation signed the Charter in June 1945; Truman signed the instrument of ratification on August 8. Fix: "…and signed the United States' ratification of the United Nations Charter."

- **John Tyler, Benjamin Harrison, Calvin Coolidge** — bullets out of chronological order, against the pattern every other card follows. Tyler runs …1842, 1842, **1844, 1843**, 1845, 1845; Harrison runs 1889, 1890×5, **1889**, 1892 (the Pan-American conference bullet is stranded after the 1890 block); Coolidge runs …1928, 1928, **1927** ("I do not choose to run" placed last).

- **Franklin D. Roosevelt** — "…he was **paralysed** by illness in 1921." — The deck's only British spelling. Fix: "paralyzed". (No other -ise/-our/-re spellings anywhere in the deck; "Ford's Theatre" is a proper noun and correct as written. No URL or image filename needs touching.)
